Research

When a neural network forecasts rain over Paris, which part of the atmosphere was it looking at?

Deep learning models now rival physics-based weather prediction, but they don’t explain themselves. I develop attribution methods that trace a forecast back to the input fields behind it, and test whether those explanations are faithful to the model, stable under perturbation and physically plausible.

Noise doesn’t blur an explanation. It moves it.

Preprint · 2026 · under review

Explanation of Dynamic Physical Field Predictions using WassersteinGrad: Application to Autoregressive Weather Forecasting

Younes Essafouri, Laure Raynaud, Luciano Drozda, Laurent Risser

Small input perturbations don’t make a weather model’s gradient explanations noisier; they shift them across the map. WassersteinGrad averages the perturbed explanations with optimal transport instead of pixel by pixel, and recovers one coherent region of influence.

EGU General Assembly 2026 · Vienna · Poster

A Framework for Explainable AI in Weather Forecasting: Diagnosing Deep Learning Models via Gradient-Based Attributions

Younes Essafouri, Corentin Seznec, Luciano Drozda, Laure Raynaud, Laurent Risser

A framework for diagnosing neural weather models with gradient attributions. Summarising each attribution map by its barycenter and a 95 % influence ellipse shows how far the model looks at each level of the atmosphere.
