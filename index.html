About

A forecast is an argument about the sky — I study whether the argument holds up.

I'm a PhD researcher at the Institut de Mathématiques de Toulouse and Université Paul Sabatier, working on explainable AI for numerical weather prediction. My research asks a simple-sounding question: when a deep learning model forecasts the weather, can we understand why — and is its reasoning physically plausible?

Before the PhD I studied at Grenoble INP — Ensimag and spent a semester at KTH in Stockholm. I've worked on physics-informed machine learning for cardiac modelling at Dassault Systèmes, neural operators for the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equations at the Hubert Curien laboratory, and a handful of smaller projects that sit between mathematics and code.

Explanation of Dynamic Physical Field Predictions using WassersteinGrad

A new attribution method built on Wasserstein barycenters that denoises gradient-based explanations for dynamic, high-dimensional physical fields — applied to deep weather forecasters where per-pixel gradients alone are noisy and unstable.

Research

Explaining deep learning weather models, one gradient at a time.

My work applies gradient-based attribution — SmoothGrad, VarGrad, and Wasserstein barycenters — to interrogate how models like GraphCast, UNETR++, AIFS and AROME translate atmospheric fields into forecasts, separating genuine physical reasoning from spurious correlation.
